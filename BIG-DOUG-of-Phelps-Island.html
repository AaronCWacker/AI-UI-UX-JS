What is authentic here, and what isn't

The three islands are transcribed, not invented

Rounds 15, 16 and 17 are parsed at load time from the arcade round maps in Andrew Schultz's Dig Dug II FAQ, which draws every round as a lattice of stakes with the fault lines that are already cut when the round begins and the coastline marked around them. The parser reproduces the stake positions, the pre-cut faults, the inland lakes and the diagonal shores. Type BigDoug.mapSource() in the console to print the raw map for the round you are on and compare it line for line against the source below.

Round 17 re-cuts the round 1 island: same silhouette, different stakes and faults. That is the FAQ's own layout, not a shortcut taken here.

6 and up15,000 · 20,000 · 30,000 · 50,000 · 80,000NES manual, unverified for arcade
  • Pooka 200. Fygar 200 popped vertically, 400 popped horizontally.
  • Enemy mix per round: 15 has 3 Fygar and 6 Pooka · 16 has 4 and 4 · 17 has 3 and 4.
  • Bonus food once three pieces of land have been cut away: round 15 pineapple 6,000, round 16 the big drink 7,000, round 17 draws at random.
  • The jackhammer only works while you stand on a stake. The crack runs from that stake to the next stake or to the water.
  • When faults divide the island, the smaller piece sinks with everything standing on it, including you.
  • Enemies cannot cross a fault as themselves. They turn into a slow mask to get through, which is your window to pump them. You cross faults freely — that asymmetry is the whole game.
  • The last enemy runs for the shore and jumps in rather than let you have the points.
  • Extra life at 30,000 and 80,000, the common dip-switch setting.
  • Turning is grid-locked, so a ninety-degree turn while out of line costs you a beat. The FAQ calls the arcade's version of this its fatal flaw. It is reproduced, slightly softened.

Openly reconstructed

Enemy starting positions are not from the ROM. The FAQ states the placements are fixed but undivinable because most enemies begin off-screen. Here they enter from the far shore, which plays correctly but is a guess.

  • Your own starting spot, walk and chase speeds, fire timing, mask duration and inflation decay are tuned by feel, not measured from the board.
  • The arcade playfield scrolls and rounds 15 and 16 are wider than one screen. The whole island is fitted into view here instead, so you can see enemies the cabinet would have hidden. That makes this easier than the original in a way the layouts alone do not.
  • Original geometry throughout. No ROM data, sprites, audio or trademarks. Namco's Dig Dug II is named only to identify the game being reconstructed.
